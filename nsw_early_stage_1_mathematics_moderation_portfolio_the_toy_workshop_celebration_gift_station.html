Teacher: "Maya, why do your robot and dog have the same volume even though the dog is much longer?"

"Because volume means how much space it takes up! My dog is flat and stretched out, but I didn't get any new blocks from the tub and I didn't throw any away. It's the exact same 10 blocks, so the amount of space is identical!"

3D Objects & Stacking
MAE-3DS-01

Predicts and justifies stacking based on flat/curved features.

 Autonomous Reasoning: Explains that a cylinder can stack or roll depending on orientation. Builds complex stable structure deliberately orienting flat faces. Taught Routine: Uses flat faces to stack blocks into a tower. Recognizes independently that a sphere rolls off and cannot stack, correcting through trial. Guided Execution: Attempts to balance cylinder on curved surface or point of prism. Requires teacher prompting to identify flat sides that balance.
Comparing Volumes
MAE-3DS-02

Understands volume as space occupied; deconstructs/rebuilds.

 Conceptual Conservation: Articulates that changing the spatial arrangement does not change the amount of space occupied: "Same 10 blocks = same volume." Direct Counting: Deconstructs tower and rebuilds a train; verifies equal volume by touching and recounting each block (1 to 8). Partial Understanding: Attempts to add extra blocks from tub; requires teacher redirection to conserve the original set of blocks.
2D Shape Tracing & Features
MAE-2DS-01

Traces 3D faces; identifies lines, curves, corners, and sides.

 High Precision & Vocabulary: Traces squares, rectangles, and circles neatly. Accurately links traced 2D shapes back to the solid 3D parent faces with correct terminology. Adequate Competence: Traces closed shapes with slight wobble; correctly names circle, square, and rectangle. Identifies straight lines versus curves. Emergent / Supported: Struggles to hold block steady; produces open lines or gaps. Confuses shapes (calls square "box") without teacher scaffolding.
Area & Superimposition
MAE-2DS-02

Compares surface area directly by superimposing shapes.

 Boundary Overhang Reasoning: Superimposes shapes aligning corners and edges; explains that the boundary overhang proves the larger surface area. Direct Superimposition: Places smaller shape on top of larger shape; states the larger shape has more space because edges are still visible. Perceptual Guessing: Selects shape based on colour or orientation; superimposes shapes crookedly without aligning boundaries until guided.
Working Mathematically
MAO-WM-01

Communicates thinking, justifies strategies and spatial relations.

 Clear, Confident Justification: Uses precise comparative vocabulary (*"takes up the exact same space"*, *"larger surface area"*). Explores challenge prompt autonomously. Sound Recounting: Explains thinking clearly using everyday and simple mathematical language (*"bigger"*, *"same blocks"*, *"curved"*). Prompted Responses: Answers with single words or everyday gestures (*"it's big"*, pointing); requires teacher scaffolding to verbalize mathematical thinking.

Stage Team Moderation Notes & Best Practice:

  • Oral Language as Primary Evidence: In Early Stage 1, student oral dialogue captured in teacher scribing blocks provides the primary evidence of mathematical reasoning, rather than written recordings.
  • Conservation of Volume vs Direct Counting: Grade B students understand that volume is conserved regardless of shape, whereas Grade C students rely on physically counting cubes to confirm equivalence.
  • Superimposition Technique: Ensure students physically place shapes directly over one another with aligned edges, rather than solely comparing them side-by-side.
