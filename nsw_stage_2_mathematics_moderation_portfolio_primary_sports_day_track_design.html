Tahlia's Critical Place Value Justification on 2.05 m vs 2.5 m: "I specifically recorded 2 metres and 5 centimetres as 2.05 m because 5 centimetres is 5 hundredths of a metre (5/100). There are zero tenths (0 × 10cm). If I mistakenly omitted the zero and wrote 2.5 m, that would equal 2 metres and 5 tenths (50cm or 2.50 m), which is 45 centimetres too long! The placeholder zero preserves the hundredths place."
Step 4: Decimal Ordering & Helper Challenge Proof
Ascending Order with Relational Symbols:

0.85 m < 2.05 m < 4.75 m < 7.50 m

Proof: Compare whole ones first (0 < 2 < 4 < 7). 0.85m has 0 ones; 2.05m has 2 ones; 4.75m has 4 ones; 7.50m has 7 ones.

Disproving the Helper's Claim (0.8 m vs 0.75 m):

"The student helper has fallen into the common 'longer decimals are bigger numbers' trap, treating decimals as disconnected whole counting numbers (thinking 75 is bigger than 8). This is mathematically incorrect because place value is determined from left to right:

0.8 m = 8 tenths = 80 hundredths = 80 cm
0.75 m = 7 tenths + 5 hundredths = 75 cm

Since 8 tenths is larger than 7 tenths (80 cm > 75 cm), 0.8 m is greater than 0.75 m! (0.8 > 0.75)."

Halfway Distance Calculation:
"Between 0.7 m (0.70 m = 70 cm) and 0.8 m (0.80 m = 80 cm), the exact middle is 75 cm. Therefore, the distance halfway is 0.75 m!"

Teacher Scribing (Verbatim Dialogue)

Teacher: "Tahlia, how do you explain why 0.25 on a 1-metre track represents 2.50 metres on your 10-metre track?"

"Because 1/4 of 1 whole is 0.25. When the whole is stretched 10 times bigger to 10 metres, every fraction is scaled by 10: 1/4 of 10 metres is 2.5 metres, or 2 metres and 50 centimetres (2.50 m). The benchmark proportion remains identical!"
