Museum Exhibit Plaque: Mathematical Proof Wall
1. General Rule for Volume of Any Rectangular Prism (In Words):

"To determine the volume of any rectangular prism, calculate the two-dimensional area of the base layer by multiplying the length by the width (which establishes the number of cubic units in a single layer), and then multiply that base area by the vertical height (the total number of identical stacked layers). In words: Volume equals length multiplied by width multiplied by height."

2. Proof of Mathematical Equivalence (Factor Triples & Associative Property):

Target Volume = 24 m³
Prism A: (4m × 3m) × 2m = 12m² × 2m = 24 m³
Prism B: (6m × 1m) × 4m = 6m² × 4m = 24 m³
Proof: By the associative property of multiplication, grouping factors differently does not alter the product: (4 × 3) × 2 = (6 × 1) × 4 = 24.

3. Mind-Blowing Cubic Centimetre Conversion:

"Visitors often mistakenly think $1\text{ m}^3 = 100\text{ cm}^3$ because $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$. Here is the geometric place-value proof:

  • 1 metre length = 100 cm blocks along the base line
  • Base layer = 100 rows × 100 columns = 10,000 cm³ blocks per single layer
  • 1 metre height = 100 vertical layers of 10,000 blocks = 1,000,000 cm³ per cubic metre!
24 m³ × 1,000,000 cm³/m³ = 24,000,000 cm³ (Twenty-Four Million Cubic Centimetres!)
Xavier's Unprompted Spatial Exploration: "Could we also design an exhibit using a fractional dimension? Yes! A prism measuring $8\text{m} \times 1.5\text{m} \times 2\text{m}$ also yields $(8 \times 1.5) \times 2 = 12 \times 2 = \mathbf{24\text{ m}^3}$. This proves dimensions do not need to be whole numbers to maintain identical volumetric capacity!"

Teacher Scribing (Verbatim Dialogue)

Teacher: "Xavier, why didn't you just turn Prism A onto its side to create your second exhibit?"

"Because rotating an object in space only changes its orientation, not its geometric structure. A $4 \times 3 \times 2$ prism turned on its side is still the exact same prism. To make a genuine museum illusion, you must decompose and recombine the factors into completely new edge lengths, like $6 \times 1 \times 4$."
