CLASS WORK • YEAR 2 Aria M. • Extensive Sample 1

School Fun Day: Puzzle Zone Challenge

Aria M. (Year 2)

OUTSTANDING DEPTH!

1. Calendar Duration (Today: 20 Oct 2026 ➔ Event: 12 March 2027)

✓ Multi-month jump reasoning!

• Full months jump: Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb = 4 full months.

• Days remaining in Oct: 31 - 20 = 11 days (1 week and 4 days).

• Days in March: 12 days (1 week and 5 days).

• Total extra days: 11 + 12 = 23 days = 3 weeks and 2 days.

Total Duration: 4 months, 3 weeks and 2 days (or exactly 20 weeks and 3 days).

"I used landmark months first because counting 143 single days on a calendar takes too long and makes errors likely!"

2. Timed Game Challenge (iPad Stopwatch Trials)

✓ Standard unit conversion
Task 1: Tangram Cat Puzzle
00:42.00 (42 seconds)
Task 2: Mirror Maze Run
00:35.00 (35 seconds)

Combined Time: 42s + 35s = 77 seconds = 1 minute and 17 seconds.

"I partitioned 77 into 60 seconds (1 whole minute) and 17 seconds. I set our game booth time limit to 90 seconds (1 minute 30 seconds). This gives players a 13-second buffer to fix mistakes."

3. Chance Prediction Under 90s Time Limit

• Task 1 alone (42s): Likely (Plenty of time compared to 90s).

• Both Tasks combined (77s): Likely (77s is 13 seconds faster than 90s).

• Completing both in under 30s: Impossible (The fastest single task is 35s).

CHALLENGE PROMPT: 15-SECOND TIME CUT

New Time Limit: 90s - 15s = 75 seconds (1 min 15s).

"Our combined trial time is 77 seconds. Because 77s is greater than 75s, winning our booth game is now UNLIKELY! Players must beat their personal best by 2 whole seconds without a single hesitation."

Chance Shift: From 'Likely' (13s buffer) ➔ 'Unlikely' (2s deficit).

Assessment Task Brief (Sequence 5)

Context: School Fun Day Challenge & Time Explorer

Design a Timed Game Challenge for the School Fun Day (Sports Arena, Art Studio, or Puzzle Zone).

  1. Calendar Planning: Count months, weeks, and days until the 2027 Fun Day event.
  2. Timed Game Challenge: Choose 2 tasks, time them in seconds/minutes with an iPad stopwatch, and calculate the combined total.
  3. Set Time Limit: Set an allowed time limit and justify why.
  4. Chance Prediction: Tag the completion likelihood as likely, unlikely, or possible.
  5. Challenge Prompt: Explain how a sudden 15-second time cut alters the likelihood of players winning your game!

NESA Common Grade Scale & Stage 1 Rubric

Key NESA Rubric Principle: Depth, Not Acceleration

Grade A reflects applying extensive knowledge and skills of Stage 1 content to unfamiliar situations. It does not require content from Stage 2.

Grade C (Sound): Demonstrates sound knowledge and skills with an adequate level of competence, using taught routines.
