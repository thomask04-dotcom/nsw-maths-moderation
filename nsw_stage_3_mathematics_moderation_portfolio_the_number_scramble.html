Phase 2: Codebreaker Strategy & Algebraic Patterns Reflection
Marcus's Structural Analysis on Forcing Values > 20:

"To systematically force numbers greater than 20 out of the small set {2, 3, 4, 5}, I identified two dominant structural patterns:

1. The Sum-Multiplier Strategy: If you perform operations strictly according to standard priority without grouping symbols, multiplication only scales a single digit (e.g. 4 × 5 = 20). However, by using grouping symbols to force an addition step first, we scale the sum of two numbers by a third factor. For example, in (5 + 4) × 3, we multiply 9 by 3, instantly reaching 27 before subtracting the smallest digit 2. The brackets act as a mathematical lever!

2. Strategic Conservation of the Smallest Digit: The digit 2 is most powerful when used as a subtractor at the very end (−2) rather than as a multiplier. If you multiply by 2, you only double, whereas multiplying by 4 or 5 scales the quantity dramatically.

Phase 3: The Bracket Removal Proof

Selected Expression: Expression B: (4 + 2) × 5 − 3 = 27

WITH Grouping Symbols:

(4 + 2) × 5 − 3

= 6 × 5 − 3

= 30 − 3

= 27

WITHOUT Grouping Symbols:

4 + 2 × 5 − 3

= 4 + 10 − 3

= 14 − 3

= 11

Marcus's Written Justification:

"Removing the brackets caused the answer to plummet from 27 down to 11 (a difference of 16!). Without brackets, the universal agreed order forces multiplication to take precedence over addition (2 × 5 = 10). The 4 is no longer grouped into the multiplier. This proves why mathematicians agreed on grouping symbols: without an agreed hierarchy, two people looking at the exact same sequence of numerals will generate conflicting mathematical realities. Brackets are mandatory to override standard operator precedence."

Teacher: "Marcus, why did (4 + 2) × 5 - 3 become so much smaller when you erased the brackets?"

"Because without brackets, the 4 is detached from the multiplication. In (4 + 2) × 5, you are making 5 copies of the entire group of 6. But in 4 + 2 × 5, you are only making 5 copies of the 2, leaving the 4 sitting as a lonely addend! Brackets change the fundamental group structure."
