Assessment Task Brief (Sequence 10)

Context: Tabletop Museum Exhibition Booth Challenge

Students work with a partner to design a tabletop Ooshie Display Booth using classroom tiles, blocks, and pattern cards.

Task Design Rules:

  1. The Booth Floor (Area): Use square tiles to build a flat floor footprint on grid paper using neat rows and columns. Measure and record total floor area.
  2. Display Pedestals (Volume): Use wooden or connecting cubes to build 3D pedestals to hold museum objects (ooshies). Build solid structures without gaps and use layering strategies to count total volume.
  3. Entrance Arch & Banner (Transformations & Symmetry): Design a banner or sign for your display using pattern blocks that shows a slide (translation) OR a line of symmetry.

Challenge Prompt (Unfamiliar / Novel Context):

"The Exhibition Expansion Challenge: A delivery of 12 MORE volume cubes and 6 MORE area tiles just arrived for your display booth! Can you make your booth bigger so that the entrance banner stays symmetrical, while adding a multi-layer pedestal without creating any empty gaps? Explain how your total area and volume changed!"

NESA Common Grade Scale & Stage 1 Rubric

Key NESA Rubric Principle: Depth, Not Acceleration

From the NSW Stage 1 Mathematics Rubric Overview (2022 Syllabus): "Grade A is about depth, not acceleration. Grade A reflects applying Stage 1 content readily to new situations. It does not require content from Stage 2." Teachers should not accelerate students into Stage 2/3 formulas to justify an 'A'. Rather, Grade A is demonstrated through autonomous reasoning, conservation of units, and spatial problem-solving in unfamiliar contexts.

Grade A (Extensive):

Extensive knowledge and understanding; readily applies knowledge and skills to new situations; very high competence and autonomous reasoning.

Grade B (Thorough):

Thorough knowledge and understanding; high competence; applies knowledge and skills to most familiar situations with clear structured communication.

Grade C (Sound):

Sound knowledge and understanding of the main areas of content; adequate competence; demonstrates skills accurately in familiar situations using taught routines.

Grade D (Basic):

Basic knowledge and understanding; limited competence; applies skills to some familiar situations, often with guidance or prompting.

Grade E (Elementary):

Elementary knowledge in few areas; very limited competence; requires substantial direct support to complete isolated task steps.
