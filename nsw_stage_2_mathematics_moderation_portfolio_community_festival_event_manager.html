Phase 2: Total Quantity Verification & Equal Sharing

Total Item Count: 16 (craft) + 4 (balls) + 12 (snacks) = 32 total prize items.

Division & Sharing Sentence: 32 ÷ 4 = 8 items per Activity Station (0 remainder).
Inverse Verification: 8 × 4 = 32 ⇔ 32 ÷ 4 = 8.
Internal Equal Distribution: Every single station receives exactly 4 Craft Kits (16 ÷ 4 = 4), 1 Sports Ball (4 ÷ 4 = 1), and 3 Snack Packs (12 ÷ 4 = 3). 4 + 1 + 3 = 8 items each!
Manager's Emergency Update ($80 Budget • 5 Activity Stations)
Ananya's Multiplicative Relational Solution: "With 5 stations, the total number of items must be a multiple of 5 so there are no remainders. I analyzed the catalogue rates:
  • Token Packs come in packs of 5 (1 × 5 = 5 items, $10). Any multiple of these is already divisible by 5.
  • Sports Balls come in packs of 2 (2 items, $8). To make a multiple of 5 items, I need 5 packs: 5 × 2 = 10 balls (5 × $8 = $40).
New Purchase Order:
• 3 Token Packs: 3 × $10 = $30 (gives 3 × 5 = 15 tokens)
• 5 Sports Ball Packs: 5 × $8 = $40 (gives 5 × 2 = 10 balls)
Budget Balance: Total spend = $30 + $40 = $70, which is comfortably under the new $80 limit! Change = $80 - $70 = $10.
Fair Distribution: Total items = 15 + 10 = 25 items.
Number sentence: 25 ÷ 5 = 5 items per station (Each station receives 3 tokens and 2 sports balls)."

Teacher: "Why did you choose constant difference to find your change instead of standard vertical subtraction?"

"Because when you have 100, you have to trade from the hundreds into the tens and then into the ones. That creates two regrouping steps where slips happen. By counting up or using 100 - 90 = 10, then 10 - 4 = 6, I decompose the problem mentally with zero trades!"
